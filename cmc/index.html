Church Maintenance Coordinator

CMC Application Suite

Campus facilities management, work order tracking, and preventive maintenance coordination for churches and ministries

The Church Maintenance Coordinator (CMC) is a facilities management system for churches and ministries. It tracks maintenance work, assets, vendors, and preventive maintenance across a multi-building campus. Below is a tour of the application, section by section, using screenshots from an example deployment — click any screenshot to view it full size.

Dashboard

The dashboard is the landing page and gives a real-time snapshot of the whole campus: six KPI cards covering active work orders, assets, buildings, floors, and locations, plus a Preventive Maintenance panel that flags overdue, due-soon, and on-track schedules at a glance.

Scrolling down surfaces the Contracts & Warranties panel, which tracks registrations, insurance policies, and other renewal-dated items before they lapse.

Campus Management

The Campus section organizes the physical property. An interactive map plots building footprints and color-coded asset markers over an aerial view — click a marker to jump straight to that asset, or click a building to see its open work orders.

Underneath the map, campus structure is broken into Buildings, Floors, and Locations. All 6 buildings are catalogued with the spaces they contain — the 1987 Building (School) houses the kitchen/cafeteria, library, and computer lab, while the 2009 Building (Church) contains the sanctuary, narthex, and nurseries. Floors span from basement level (-1) up to level 2, and named sub-areas like the Organ Enclosure can be tracked as Locations for granular maintenance targeting.

Work Orders

Work orders are the heart of day-to-day maintenance coordination, and CMC offers three views onto the same data. The Board view is a Kanban layout with Open, In Progress, On Hold, and Completed columns, so status changes are just a drag away.

The List view lays the same work orders out as a sortable table — status, priority, assignee, and due/done dates all in one row, with color-coded status dots for quick scanning.

And the Calendar view plots work orders on their due dates across the month, useful for spotting scheduling conflicts before they happen.

Clicking into any work order opens a detail modal for logging labor hours, parts and vendor costs, completion notes, and before/after photos.

Asset Management

Every piece of equipment worth tracking — HVAC units, vehicles, generators, and more — lives in the Asset list, 55 items strong, each carrying a category, location, criticality rating, and active/retired status.

Each asset has its own detail page with make/model, serial number, map coordinates, a maintenance point of contact, and a full work history.

Recurring upkeep is handled by Maintenance Schedules, which trigger on a time interval (e.g. "every 3 months") and automatically generate work orders as they come due — including schedules flagged for compliance.

Fleet vehicles get their own tab tracking VIN, plate, and the three dates that matter most: registration, insurance, and inspection expiry.

Vendors & Reporting

The Vendors & Contacts page keeps outside contractors organized — rate, Certificate of Insurance (COI) expiry with a days-remaining countdown, and contract expiration, all in one table.

Reports round things out with spend & forecast trends, preventive-maintenance compliance analytics, and an Expiry Board that consolidates every warranty, COI, contract, and vehicle renewal date approaching within 60 days into one prioritized list.

Configuration & Settings

General Settings controls org-wide basics — facility name, logo, address, maintenance contact email, brand colors, and locale/timezone defaults that apply to every user immediately.

User Management lists everyone with access and their role — admin, technician, trustee, or requester — with inline role changes and account deactivation, all governed by role-based permissions.
